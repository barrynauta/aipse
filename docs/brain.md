# The brain

AIpse uses the brain as a map. Not because software is a brain, but because the brain is a good list of what a self needs: a place for knowledge, a memory of events, feelings, a body, a conscience, a voice, and things that run by themselves.

For each part: what it does in the brain, and what it means for AIpse.

## Cortex

**In the brain.** The outer layer. Long-term knowledge, language, facts, the things you know without remembering when you learned them.

**In AIpse.** The knowledge base: markdown files, searched quickly. It is the information source. Facts, not feelings. This is the *idem* part of you.

## Hippocampus

**In the brain.** Turns experiences into memories with a when and a where. Without it, you know facts but not your own story.

**In AIpse.** A memory of events over time, for example a temporal knowledge graph. It answers "when did this change?" and "what happened around that time?".

## Limbic system

**In the brain.** The emotional core. It gives memories weight, makes some things matter and others not, and carries attachment and fear.

**In AIpse.** Three things. **Moments**: what happened and how you felt, with words from the Feeling Wheel and a strength. **People**: who was in a moment, and how close they are to you. **Mood**: AIpse's own state, named but never acted on. Everything here ages.

## Insula

**In the brain.** Feels the body from the inside: heartbeat, hunger, tiredness. It is why you are irritated before you know you are hungry.

**In AIpse.** One short summary a day, in words: sleep, recovery, sitting and moving, training, food and water. No raw data, and no nudging. It reads the body, it does not coach it.

## Prefrontal cortex

**In the brain.** Plans, weighs, and tells the limbic system "not now". The last part of the brain to finish growing, as every teenager proves.

**In AIpse.** The conscience, built on values you write down yourself (see [values](values.md)). It speaks up in one line and one question, and it can hold an outgoing message until you confirm.

## Broca's area

**In the brain.** Turns thoughts into speech. Damage it, and you know what you want to say but cannot say it.

**In AIpse.** Everything AIpse says to you: the evening digest, the mood line, the check-ins, the conscience. One character, your language, plain and dry.

## Thalamus

**In the brain.** The relay station. Almost every signal passes through it on its way to the right part of the brain.

**In AIpse.** The places where your answers and signals come in and get sent on: a chat bot, your phone, a terminal. They all end up in the same place.

## Nerves

**In the brain.** Afferent nerves carry signals in, from the senses and the body. Efferent nerves carry signals out, to the muscles.

**In AIpse.** In: what AIpse reads, mostly your own words and what the body sources say. Out: what AIpse says back. It says it only to you, never to anyone else.

## Autonomic system

**In the brain.** Heartbeat, breathing, digestion. Nobody decides to do them.

**In AIpse.** The scheduled jobs that run by themselves: the evening digest, the processing of replies, the night. One pause switch stops all of it.

## Sleep

**In the brain.** Sleep is when the day is replayed and the cortex keeps what matters. Memories fade or settle overnight.

**In AIpse.** A nightly pass: decide again how much each thing still counts, close what went unanswered, settle the day's confirmed moments into memory, and decide AIpse's own mood for tomorrow.

## Plasticity

**In the brain.** The brain rewires itself through what you do again and again. Not in one big change, but in many small ones.

**In AIpse.** Kaizen, balanced by the tomoe. Once a week, one step too small to fail, aimed at whichever of mind, spirit and body lags. Once a month, a view of how the three moved. See [kaizen and the tomoe](kaizen-and-the-tomoe.md).

## What a brain has that AIpse does not, yet

A real memory knows when it does not know. Ask someone about a person they met twice, and they will tell you it is not much to go on. AIpse should do the same: every answer about a person or a feeling should say how much it rests on. A few old moments is thin. Many recent ones is solid. See Socrates in [philosophy](philosophy.md).

A real memory also wakes up by cue, not by date. You remember to ask someone a question when you see them, not because a list told you on Tuesday. And surprise makes things stick: a hard moment with someone who is usually warm matters more than another hard moment with someone who is often difficult. Both are directions, not built parts.
