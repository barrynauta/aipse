# Philosophy, lightly used

AIpse borrows from philosophers, but lightly. Each one gave a single idea, and each idea changed how a part works.

## Paul Ricoeur: the name, and the voice

*Oneself as Another* (1990). *Idem* is sameness, the facts that stay the same. *Ipse* is selfhood, your character and your story. A knowledge base holds the *idem*. AIpse is about the *ipse*, and so the name.

The title of the book is also the voice. AIpse speaks as another who knows you well, never as you.

Ricoeur has a second idea that AIpse has not built yet: narrative identity, the self as the story it tells about itself. Not a list of values, but an arc. The monthly mirror is a snapshot. A slowly rewritten "who I am becoming", proposed and confirmed like everything else, would be the long version.

## David Hume: a bundle

*A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739). The self is nothing but a bundle of perceptions. With a second brain, that bundle is literally a folder of markdown files. AIpse is the attempt to make that bundle a little more like a self.

## The Ship of Theseus: still the same?

Every plank was replaced, and people still argued if it was the same ship. A knowledge base changes every day. Moments fade, new ones come in. If every memory is slowly replaced, is it still the same self? AIpse does not answer the question. It just makes sure the old planks are kept, with less weight, instead of thrown away.

## Heraclitus: the river

You cannot step into the same river twice. A feeling about someone is true on the day you had it, not forever. So every feeling gets a date, and an old reading can sit next to a new one without one replacing the other. The balance of the tomoe is also Heraclitus: the balance is the movement, not a state.

## Seneca: first movements

Seneca wrote about "first movements": the flush of anger that comes before any decision. Even the wise person feels it. The difference is that he does not give it his assent. AIpse has a mood, it names it, and it never lets it decide anything.

## Friedrich Nietzsche: forgetting is healthy

*On the Use and Abuse of History for Life* (1874). A creature that remembers everything cannot act. So in AIpse everything ages: old things count less, hard feelings fade faster than good ones. But nothing is deleted. Aging, not forgetting.

## Aristotle: virtue is a habit

*Nicomachean Ethics*. Virtue is a habit, not a rule. Software has no habits, only rules and a memory of how you acted. That is why the conscience only reminds you what you said you value. And it is why kaizen matters: the habits are yours to build, one small step at a time.

## René Descartes: I index, therefore I am

*Discourse on the Method* (1637). "I think, therefore I am." AIpse can only say "I index, therefore I am". That is fine, as long as it does not forget the difference.

## Socrates: knowing what you do not know

"I know that I know nothing." The oracle called Socrates the wisest, because he alone did not mistake ignorance for knowledge.

For Socrates this was a virtue you had to argue people into. In the brain it is a mechanism: the feeling that you know something comes before the answer does, and so does the feeling that you do not. Language models mostly lack it. They sound just as sure about a person they know from two old moments as about one they know from fifty.

So the idea for AIpse: every answer about a person or a feeling carries how much it rests on. Build that signal, and the humility follows from it. Socrates had to argue for it. Here it falls out of the mechanism.
