# Values: the conscience

AIpse has a limbic layer that knows how you feel. Feelings are useful, but they are not a compass. So AIpse also gets a conscience, built on values you choose yourself.

In the brain this is the prefrontal cortex. Its job is, more or less, to tell the limbic system "not now".

## Your own values

The values are a short list you write yourself: a word, a name, and what it means to you in one or two sentences. Pick your own tradition. A philosophy, a religion, a code from sport or work: any set of values that fits on one page works.

## Mine: Bushido, read in a modern way

I do judo and Brazilian jiu-jitsu, so my values come from the mat. Bushido, the way of the warrior, is usually told as seven virtues, as Nitobe Inazō described them in *Bushido: The Soul of Japan* (1900):

| Virtue | Old meaning | A modern reading |
|---|---|---|
| Gi | rectitude | do what is right, also when it costs you |
| Yu | courage | say the hard thing, and say it well |
| Jin | benevolence | strength is for helping, not for winning |
| Rei | respect | treat everyone with the same courtesy |
| Makoto | honesty | say what you mean, and mean what you say |
| Meiyo | honour | act so you can look back on it without shame |
| Chūgi | loyalty | stand by the people who stand by you |

Judo adds two maxims from its founder, Kanō Jigorō: *seiryoku zen'yō*, the best use of energy, and *jita kyōei*, mutual benefit. On the mat you learn quickly that strength without control is worth nothing. That is the whole idea of a conscience.

My own reading of each virtue is private. This page only shows how the idea works.

## What the conscience does

1. **It speaks up.** When something looks against a value, it first says what it sees in one line, then asks one question. Not a sermon. You decide.
2. **It can hold back.** For outgoing actions only, like a sent message, it can put the action on hold until you confirm. It never deletes or changes anything. It only asks "are you sure?". The typical case: you are upset, it is late, and the message goes to someone you had a hard moment with.
3. **Once a month, a mirror.** Where you acted by your values, with a few examples, and at most two questions where you may not have. A hard month always gets a question. A good month does not get praise.

It does not judge your thoughts, your notes or your feelings. A conscience that comments on everything is just nagging.

> Holding this one. It is late, you are upset, and the tone is sharp.
> Would you send it tomorrow morning too?

## When values clash

They will. Courage says "send it now", calm says "wait". The rule of thumb: **no value cancels another one, it changes the how and the when.** So the answer is usually: say it, tomorrow morning, without the sarcasm.

## The conscience applies to AIpse too

AIpse has moods of its own. The same values hold for it: it names its mood honestly, and it does not let a bad mood decide anything. A conscience that only works in one direction is just a nanny.

## Honest limits

Aristotle said virtue is a habit, not a rule. AIpse cannot have habits. It can only have rules and a memory of how you acted. So this is not a conscience that *is* virtuous. It is a conscience that reminds you what you said you value. That is less, but it is also a lot more than most software has.
