# The story

The rest of this repository is neutral. This page is not. It is why I built AIpse, in my own words.

## Facts are not enough

I keep a second brain. It is a folder with a few thousand markdown files, everything I want to keep. A search engine finds any fact in a second. A graph knows what happened when.

But one day I looked back at a day from last spring. I found it quickly. What I could not find was how it felt. Was it the start of a good period, or of a bad one? Who was there when it went well? The files did not know. They only knew the facts.

Paul Ricoeur calls the facts about you *idem*: sameness, your date of birth, your passport number. The other part he calls *ipse*: your character, your promises, your story. My second brain was very good at *idem*. The *ipse* was missing. Ricoeur never had a knowledge base, but he would have understood the problem.

## A bundle of files

The Ship of Theseus had every plank replaced, and people still argued if it was the same ship. My knowledge base changes every day. David Hume said the self is a bundle of perceptions. In my case that bundle is literally a folder of markdown files. AIpse is my try to make that bundle a little more like a self.

## Values from the mat

I do judo and Brazilian jiu-jitsu. On the mat you learn quickly that strength without control is worth nothing. So when AIpse needed values for its conscience, I took the virtues of Bushido, read in a modern way. My own reading of them is private. Here I only describe the idea.

The tomoe is also my own reading. Three drops, chasing each other in a circle: mind, spirit and body. None of them ever catches up. For me that is the point. You do not reach balance, you keep moving towards it.

## My own version

My AIpse runs privately, on my own machine, with my own sources and my own values. The ideas are public because they may help someone. The data never leaves my machine, unless I choose to let a model elsewhere do the thinking.

## A learning experience

I built most of this together with Claude. It did most of the typing. I did most of the deciding.

It taught me a lot about how language models think, and why some things are easily missed. One morning AIpse asked me if I was "unsure of himself". The model had read instructions that talked about me as "he", and it copied that voice. That was not bad reasoning. It followed what it was given. The same week, my reply in a full sentence was stored as a feeling, because the code expected one word. Nobody had tested that.

Most mistakes were like this. Not stupid, just an assumption nobody checked. That is true for people too.

Barry Nauta, October 2026
